How the cover is worked out

PartAmount

How much term insurance do I need?

Term insurance pays a lump sum to your family if you die during the policy term. The right amount is what your family would need to keep living as they do now, repay your loans and still reach their big goals. This calculator works that out from your own numbers rather than a rule of thumb.

Cover = present value of family expenses + loans + goals − savings − existing cover Present value of expenses = yearly expenses × Σ ((1 + inflation) ÷ (1 + return))t for each year t the family needs support. It is the sum that, invested at the return you enter, can pay expenses that rise with inflation for those years.

Worked example

Say your family spends ₹6,00,000 a year without you and needs support for 30 years. With 6% inflation and a 7% return on the payout, they need ₹1,57,60,723 today to cover those expenses. Add ₹30,00,000 of loans and ₹20,00,000 for goals, take away ₹5,00,000 of savings, and you need about ₹2,02,60,723. That rounds up to a ₹2,25,00,000 policy.

Tips for choosing cover

  • Use the family’s expenses, not your income. Leave out your own personal spending, but include rent or home costs, school fees and insurance premiums.
  • Count every loan. If you die, your family may have to repay them, so cover them in full.
  • Review every few years. Cover needs change with marriage, children, a new home loan or a big rise in income.
  • Keep term and investments separate. A plain term plan gives the most cover for the premium; investment-linked policies cost more for the same cover.

Frequently asked questions

How much term insurance cover should I have?

A common rule of thumb is 10 to 15 times your yearly income. A needs-based figure like this calculator’s is better, because it looks at your family’s actual expenses, your loans, your goals and what you have already saved.

What is term insurance?

It is pure life insurance. If you die during the term, the insurer pays the sum assured to your nominee. A plain term plan pays nothing if you outlive the term, which is why it gives a large cover for a low premium.

Do I need term insurance if no one depends on me?

Usually you need little or none. Cover is mainly for people who rely on your income, or to repay loans that someone else would otherwise have to pay.

Are term insurance premiums tax-deductible?

Under the old tax regime, premiums can be deducted under Section 80C within the ₹1.5 lakh limit. The new regime does not allow it. The payout to your nominee on death is generally tax-free.
