SIP vs Lumpsum: Which Way Should You Invest?

Updated November 2026

When you invest in a mutual fund you can put in a large amount at once (lumpsum) or invest a fixed sum every month (SIP). Neither is always better. The right choice depends on when your money arrives and how you handle market ups and downs.

Key takeaways

  • A SIP suits regular income. A lumpsum suits money you already have, such as a bonus or maturity amount.
  • SIPs average your purchase cost over time, but averaging does not guarantee a profit.
  • Staying invested for long matters more than trying to time the market.

What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly. It builds discipline, fits a salary cycle and lets you start with a small amount.

Rupee-cost averaging in simple terms

When the price is low your fixed amount buys more units, and when it is high it buys fewer. Over time your average cost per unit tends to be lower than the simple average of prices.

Example: you invest ₹10,000 in each of three months when the NAV is ₹100, ₹125 and ₹80. You buy 100 + 80 + 125 = 305 units for ₹30,000. Your average cost is ₹30,000 ÷ 305 = about ₹98.36 per unit, while the simple average NAV is ₹101.67.

This is only a way of spreading your entry points. It does not protect you from losses if prices keep falling.

When a lumpsum may make sense

If you already hold a large sum, keeping it idle has a cost. Investing it all at once puts every rupee to work from day one. The risk is that you invest just before a fall. Some investors reduce that risk by staying in a lower-risk fund first and moving money across in instalments.

Step-up SIP

A step-up SIP increases your instalment by a fixed percentage every year, in line with your growing income. This matters because a flat SIP loses importance as your salary and costs rise.

Worked example (illustrative)

These figures assume 12% a year, only to show the mechanics. Returns are not guaranteed and real returns will vary.

  • SIP: ₹10,000 a month for 15 years means you invest ₹18 lakh and would reach about ₹50.5 lakh.
  • Step-up SIP: start at ₹10,000 and raise it 10% each year. You invest about ₹38.1 lakh in total and would reach about ₹86.8 lakh.
  • Lumpsum: ₹18 lakh invested on day one would reach about ₹1.08 crore.

The lumpsum looks far bigger only because all ₹18 lakh was invested from the start. The SIP money goes in gradually. The comparison is only fair when you actually have the lumpsum available.

Time in the market

Most of the growth in long-term investing comes from compounding, which needs years. Pausing your SIP during a fall means missing the cheaper units. A longer horizon also gives more room to recover from bad years. Match risk to your time frame: money needed within a few years should not sit in volatile equity funds.

Frequently asked questions

Is SIP safer than lumpsum?

A SIP spreads your entry over time, which reduces the risk of a badly timed single purchase, but it does not remove market risk.

Can I stop or pause a SIP?

Most mutual funds allow you to pause or stop a SIP without a penalty, though some funds may charge an exit load on recent units. Check the fund terms.

How much should I invest in a SIP?

Many people begin with an amount they can sustain through bad months, then step it up as income rises. Keep an emergency fund separate.

Do SIPs guarantee returns?

No. Mutual fund returns are market linked and can be negative, especially over short periods.
