How much should an emergency fund be?

An emergency fund is money kept aside only for real emergencies: losing your job, a medical bill insurance doesn’t cover, or an urgent home or car repair. Its size depends on how much you need to get through a month and how many months you want to be covered for.

Emergency fund = (essential monthly expenses + EMIs) × months of cover Months to build it = (target − amount already set aside) ÷ what you can add each month, rounded up. Returns on the fund are ignored because it should be kept somewhere safe rather than invested for growth.

Worked example

Say your essential expenses are ₹50,000 a month and your EMIs are ₹15,000. Six months of cover is (₹50,000 + ₹15,000) × 6 = ₹3,90,000. If you have ₹1,00,000 set aside and add ₹10,000 a month, you will reach the target in 29 months.

Where to keep it

  • Easy to reach: a savings account, a sweep-in FD linked to it, or a liquid or overnight fund that you can withdraw from within a day.
  • Low risk: avoid equity funds or shares, which can fall just when you need the money.
  • Kept separate: a separate account makes it less tempting to dip into for everyday spending.
  • Topped up: refill it after you use it, and raise the target when your expenses grow.

Frequently asked questions

How many months of expenses should I keep?

Three to six months is common for salaried people with a steady job. Keep 9 to 12 months if your income is irregular, you are self-employed, yours is the only income in the family or you have dependants with health needs.

Should I include EMIs in the emergency fund?

Yes. EMIs must be paid even if you lose your income, so include them along with your essential monthly expenses.

Is health insurance a substitute for an emergency fund?

No. Health insurance covers hospital bills, but not job loss, home repairs or the costs it excludes. You need both.

Should I invest my emergency fund for higher returns?

Keep it safe and easy to reach rather than chasing returns. A liquid fund or sweep-in FD earns a little more than a savings account without much risk.
