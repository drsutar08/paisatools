How to calculate a discount

Multiply the price by the discount percentage to get the amount off, and subtract it from the price. When a second discount is offered, it is usually taken off the already reduced price, so the two percentages do not simply add up.

Price you pay = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100) × (1 − extra discount ÷ 100) Total discount = 1 − (1 − first discount) × (1 − extra discount). For example, 50% + 20% off is 1 − 0.5 × 0.8 = 60% off, not 70%.

Worked example

Say a jacket costs ₹5,000 and is on sale at 40% off, with an extra 10% off at checkout. The 40% takes off ₹2,000, leaving ₹3,000. The extra 10% is taken from ₹3,000, so it takes off ₹300. You pay ₹2,700 and save ₹2,300, which is 46% off in total, not 50%.

Points to note

  • Check the starting price: a discount is only as good as the price it starts from. Compare the final price across shops, not the percentage.
  • GST: in India, the MRP and sale prices shown to shoppers already include GST, so you do not pay it on top.
  • Flat cashback: a fixed amount off, such as ₹500, can be worth more or less than a percentage. Subtract it from the price you pay to compare.
  • No-cost EMI: these offers sometimes come instead of an upfront discount, so compare the total you pay both ways.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount percentage?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide by the original price and multiply by 100. A ₹1,000 item selling for ₹750 is 25% off.

Is 50% + 20% off the same as 70% off?

No. The extra 20% is taken off the price after the first 50%, so the total is 60% off. A straight 70% discount would be cheaper.

How do I find the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If ₹600 is the price after 40% off, the original price was ₹600 ÷ 0.6 = ₹1,000.

Is GST added to the discounted price?

For goods sold to consumers in India, the MRP and sale prices include GST, so the price shown is what you pay. Some services and business invoices show GST separately.
