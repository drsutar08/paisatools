How is the SIP for a goal worked out?

The calculator works backwards from your goal. It first finds what the goal will cost on the target date, then the monthly amount that grows to that sum at your expected return. It uses the same method as our SIP calculator, so the two always agree.

Monthly SIP = Goal ÷ ( ((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r × (1 + r) ) r is the monthly return (yearly return ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. Each SIP is invested at the start of the month. With inflation, the goal becomes Goal × (1 + inflation)years. With a yearly step-up, the starting SIP is found the same way, allowing for the higher amounts in later years.

Worked example

To build ₹1 crore in 15 years at 12% a year, you need a SIP of about ₹19,819 a month. You invest ₹35,67,352 in total, and the rest, about ₹64,32,648, comes from returns.

Ways to make a goal easier to reach

  • Start earlier. More years of compounding lower the monthly amount sharply. The note under the result shows what a five-year delay costs.
  • Step up every year. Raising the SIP with your salary lets you start smaller.
  • Use a realistic return. Returns are not guaranteed. Planning with a lower figure leaves room for bad years.
  • Review once a year. If returns fall short, increase the SIP rather than waiting until the end.

Frequently asked questions

How much SIP do I need for ₹1 crore?

It depends on the time you have and the return you assume. At 12% a year, you need about ₹19,800 a month for 15 years, about ₹10,000 a month for 20 years, or about ₹43,000 a month for 10 years. With a 10% yearly step-up, the 15-year SIP can start at about ₹11,500.

Should I adjust my goal for inflation?

Yes, for goals more than a few years away. Something that costs ₹20 lakh today would cost about ₹35.8 lakh in 10 years if prices rise 6% a year.

What is a step-up SIP?

You raise your SIP by a fixed percentage every year, usually in line with your salary. For the same goal it lowers the amount you need to start with.

What if returns are lower than expected?

Then you may fall short of the goal. Use a conservative return, review the plan every year and increase the SIP if needed. Mutual fund returns are not guaranteed.
