How does Kisan Vikas Patra work?

Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP) is a government savings certificate sold at post offices. Despite the name, anyone can buy it. Your investment doubles over a fixed term, which depends on the interest rate when you buy: at 7.5% a year it takes 115 months. Interest compounds every year and is paid out with your investment at maturity.

Months to double = log 2 ÷ log (1 + rate) × 12 At 7.5%, that is 115 months, or 9 years and 7 months. The value at maturity is twice what you invest. The value in earlier years grows by (1 + rate) each year.

Worked example

Say you invest ₹50,000 in KVP at 7.5% a year. It doubles to ₹1,00,000 after 115 months (9 years and 7 months), so you earn ₹50,000 in interest.

Key rules

  • How much: at least ₹1,000, in multiples of ₹100, with no upper limit. A PAN is needed for investments above ₹50,000, and proof of income above ₹10 lakh.
  • Early withdrawal: allowed after two years and six months, at a value set by the rules, which is less than at maturity. Earlier withdrawal is allowed only in cases such as the death of the holder.
  • Tax: there is no deduction for the investment, and the interest is taxable.
  • Other uses: a KVP can be pledged as security for a loan and transferred in certain cases.

Frequently asked questions

How long does KVP take to double?

At the current 7.5% rate, 115 months, which is 9 years and 7 months. The term is fixed by the rate on the day you buy.

Does KVP give any tax benefit?

No. There is no deduction for the amount you invest, and the interest is taxable as part of your income.

Can I withdraw from KVP early?

Yes, after two years and six months, but you get a lower value than at maturity. Before that, early withdrawal is allowed only in limited cases, such as the death of the holder.

What is the current KVP interest rate?

7.5% a year for October to December 2026. The government reviews small savings rates every quarter, so check India Post for the latest rate before you invest.
