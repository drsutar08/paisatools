What is XIRR and how is it worked out?

XIRR (extended internal rate of return) is the yearly return on a series of investments and withdrawals made on different dates. Unlike a simple percentage gain, it allows for how long each rupee was invested, so money added recently counts for less than money invested years ago.

Σ cash flow ÷ (1 + XIRR)days ÷ 365 = 0 Investments count as negative cash flows, and withdrawals and today’s value as positive ones. Days are counted from your first transaction. The calculator finds the rate that makes the total zero.

Worked example

Say you invested ₹50,000 in January 2022, ₹25,000 in July 2022, ₹40,000 in March 2023 and ₹30,000 in January 2024, and withdrew ₹20,000 in September 2024. If the investment is worth ₹1,60,000 on 1 November 2026, your XIRR is 5.93% a year.

Reading your XIRR

  • Compare it with other options. XIRR is a yearly rate, so you can set it against an FD rate or a fund’s CAGR on the same footing.
  • Short periods can mislead. Over a few months, a small gain can show up as a very high or very low yearly rate.
  • Use exact dates. For SIPs, enter each instalment date, or use the SIP calculator for a regular monthly plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is XIRR?

It is the yearly return on investments made at different dates and amounts, such as SIPs, top-ups and withdrawals. It is the rate at which all your cash flows, including today’s value, add up to zero once each is adjusted for its date.

How is XIRR different from CAGR?

CAGR suits a single investment held for a period. XIRR handles several investments and withdrawals at different times. For a single investment held to today, the two give the same answer.

Why is my XIRR different from the fund’s published return?

A fund’s published return assumes one investment at the start of the period. Your XIRR depends on when you actually put money in and took it out.

Can XIRR be negative?

Yes. If today’s value plus what you have withdrawn is less than what you invested, the XIRR is negative.
