Tax Saving for Senior Citizens in India

Updated November 2026

Retirement income usually comes from pension, bank interest and perhaps some rent or dividends. Senior citizens get some extra tax relief, but most of it sits in the old regime. This guide explains the main points and how to compare regimes. You can run your own numbers on our senior citizen income tax calculator. Rules change, so check the latest rules before you file.

Key takeaways

  • Under the old regime, the tax-free limit is higher for seniors, while the new regime slabs are the same for all ages.
  • Interest deductions, health insurance and the standard deduction on pension can reduce taxable income.
  • Compare both regimes every year, because the better one can change.

Who counts as a senior citizen

A senior citizen is aged 60 to 79 and a super senior citizen is 80 or above. These two groups get different limits under the old regime.

Higher exemption under the old regime

Under the old regime the tax-free limit is ₹3 lakh for seniors and ₹5 lakh for super seniors, and income above that is taxed at 5%, 20% and 30%. There is also a rebate under Section 87A: no tax if taxable income is up to ₹5 lakh. The new regime has the same slabs for everyone, but a much higher rebate limit.

Section 80TTB: interest deduction

Seniors can deduct up to ₹50,000 of interest earned on savings accounts, fixed deposits and recurring deposits with banks, post offices and co-operative banks. This is available only under the old regime.

Section 80D: health insurance

The deduction for health insurance under Section 80D goes up to ₹50,000 for seniors. Section 80DDB gives higher limits for certain medical costs. Keep your premium receipts and policy papers.

Standard deduction on pension

Pension is taxed like salary, and you can claim a standard deduction of ₹50,000 against it under the old regime. The new regime has its own standard deduction figure, so check the latest rules.

TDS on interest and Form 15H

Banks deduct TDS on interest above ₹1 lakh a year for seniors. If your total tax for the year will be nil, you can submit Form 15H to the bank so that no TDS is cut on your interest. Submit it at the start of each financial year, and check the latest rules for the conditions. You may still have to file a return.

Worked example (illustrative)

A senior citizen has a pension of ₹6,00,000 and ₹1,50,000 of FD interest, with no other income or deductions.

  • Old regime: after the ₹50,000 standard deduction and ₹50,000 under 80TTB, taxable income is ₹6,50,000 and tax including 4% cess is about ₹41,600.
  • New regime: taxable income is ₹6,75,000, which is within the rebate limit, so tax is ₹0.

Here the new regime is clearly better. With larger deductions, such as health insurance and 80C investments, or a super senior citizen's higher exemption, the old regime can come out ahead. That is why a yearly comparison matters. The same logic applies to anyone, and you can compare regimes for other incomes with the income tax calculator.

Ways seniors can reduce tax

  • Use Section 80TTB if you stay in the old regime.
  • Claim health insurance and medical costs under 80D and 80DDB.
  • Check TDS on your interest and use Form 15H if your total tax is nil.
  • Compare both regimes every year.

If your income is above the basic exemption limit, you generally need to file a return even if the tax is nil. Seniors with only pension and interest from one bank may be exempt in some cases. Ask a tax professional if unsure.

Frequently asked questions

Does the new regime have a higher limit for seniors?

No. The new regime slabs are the same for all ages. Seniors get higher exemption limits only under the old regime.

What is Section 80TTB?

It lets seniors deduct up to ₹50,000 of interest earned on savings accounts, fixed deposits and recurring deposits. It is available only under the old regime.

What is Form 15H?

Seniors whose total tax is nil can submit Form 15H to the bank so that no TDS is cut on their interest.

Is pension taxable?

Yes. Pension is taxed like salary, and you can claim the standard deduction against it.

Do I have to file a return if my tax is nil?

If your income is above the basic exemption limit, you generally need to file a return even if the tax is nil. Check with a professional.
