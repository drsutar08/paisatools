How does inflation change the value of money?

Inflation is the rate at which prices rise. When prices go up, the same rupee buys less. So something that costs ₹1 lakh today will cost more in future, and ₹1 lakh kept aside will buy less than it does now. This calculator shows both sides.

Future cost = Amount × (1 + inflation)years Buying power of today’s amount in future = Amount ÷ (1 + inflation)years. Years for prices to double = log 2 ÷ log (1 + inflation). The inflation rate is entered as a decimal, so 6% is 0.06.

Worked example

At 6% inflation, something that costs ₹1,00,000 today will cost about ₹1,79,085 in 10 years. Put the other way, ₹1,00,000 then will buy only about as much as ₹55,839 does today.

Using inflation in your plans

  • Inflate your goals. A goal 10 or 20 years away will cost much more than it does today. Use the future cost when you plan savings for it.
  • Judge returns after inflation. An investment only grows your wealth if it earns more than inflation after tax.
  • Some costs rise faster. Education and healthcare often go up faster than general prices, so use a higher rate for those goals.

Frequently asked questions

What inflation rate should I use?

For general living costs, many people use 5% to 6% a year. Costs such as education and healthcare often rise faster than general inflation. The RBI’s target for consumer price inflation is 4%, within a band of 2% to 6%.

What does buying power mean?

It is what a sum of money can actually buy. At 6% inflation, ₹1 lakh in 10 years will buy only about as much as ₹55,839 does today.

How long do prices take to double?

For a quick estimate, divide 72 by the inflation rate. At 6%, prices double in about 12 years; the exact figure the calculator shows is 11.9 years.

How can my savings keep up with inflation?

They need to earn more than inflation after tax. A deposit that earns less than inflation loses buying power each year, even though the rupee amount grows.
