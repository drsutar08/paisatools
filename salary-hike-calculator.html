How to calculate a salary hike

A hike is a percentage of your current salary. Multiply your salary by the hike and add it on to get the new salary. To find the hike in a job offer, divide the difference by your current salary.

New salary = current salary × (1 + hike ÷ 100)
Hike % = (new salary − current salary) ÷ current salary × 100 Real hike = (1 + hike) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. It shows how much more your salary can actually buy. The projection assumes the same hike and inflation every year.

Worked example

Say you earn ₹12,00,000 a year and get a 10% hike. Your new salary is ₹13,20,000, a raise of ₹1,20,000 a year or ₹10,000 a month. With inflation at 6%, the real hike is 3.77%: that is how much more your salary can actually buy.

Points to note

  • Compare like with like: a CTC figure includes employer PF, gratuity and bonuses, so compare CTC with CTC, not with your in-hand pay.
  • Take-home rises by less: income tax goes up, and so does PF if it is linked to your basic pay. Use the salary in-hand calculator to see the monthly figure.
  • Variable pay: if part of the new salary is a performance bonus, count only what you are fairly sure to receive.
  • Beat inflation: a hike below inflation means your salary buys less than last year, even though the number is bigger.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a salary hike percentage?

Subtract your current salary from the new salary, divide by the current salary and multiply by 100. For example, going from ₹10 lakh to ₹12 lakh is a 20% hike.

What is a good salary hike in India?

It depends on the industry and your performance, but a hike above the inflation rate means your pay is growing in real terms. Changing jobs often brings a bigger hike than a yearly appraisal.

Does a hike raise my in-hand salary by the same percentage?

Usually not. Tax goes up, and in a higher slab a larger share of the raise goes in tax. Your PF contribution can also rise if your basic pay goes up.

What is a real salary hike?

It is the hike after allowing for inflation. With a 10% hike and 6% inflation, your real hike is about 3.8%, because prices rose too.
