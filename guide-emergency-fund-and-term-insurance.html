Emergency Fund and Term Insurance: The First Two Steps of a Money Plan

Before you start investing, two things protect everything else you build: an emergency fund for short-term shocks, and term insurance for the worst case. Without them, a job loss or a big bill can force you to sell investments at a bad time or take on expensive debt. This guide explains how much you need of each and where to keep it.

Key takeaways

  • Keep 3 to 6 months of essential expenses plus EMIs as an emergency fund, or more if your income is irregular or you are the only earner.
  • Keep it safe and easy to reach: a savings account, a sweep-in FD or a liquid fund, not shares.
  • If anyone depends on your income, buy a plain term plan. A common starting point is 10 to 15 times your yearly income; a needs-based figure is better.
  • Buy health insurance too: an emergency fund alone is not enough for a hospital bill.

How big should your emergency fund be?

Add up the expenses you cannot cut, such as rent, groceries, school fees, insurance premiums and utilities, plus your EMIs. Multiply by the number of months you want to cover: 3 months if you have a stable job and a working partner, 6 months for most people, and 9 to 12 months if you are self-employed or the only earner.

Where to keep it

  • Savings account: instant access but a low rate. Keep about a month’s expenses here.
  • Sweep-in or auto FD: earns FD interest and breaks automatically when you need the money.
  • Liquid fund: usually earns a little more than a savings account, with the money back in a working day.
  • Not in shares or equity funds: they can be down just when you need the money.

How much term insurance do you need?

Term insurance pays your family a lump sum if you die during the policy term, and nothing if you outlive it, which is why it is cheap. The cover should replace the income your family would lose: enough to pay off loans, meet their living costs until they are independent, and fund big goals such as your children’s education. A quick rule is 10 to 15 times your yearly income; the term insurance calculator works it out from your actual expenses, loans and savings.

Choosing a term plan

  • Cover until your dependants are independent, usually up to age 60 or 65.
  • Buy early: the premium is fixed when you buy, and it is lower while you are young and healthy.
  • Disclose your health and habits honestly, so a claim is not rejected later.
  • Keep insurance and investing separate: endowment and money-back plans give far less cover for the same premium.
  • Check the insurer’s claim settlement record, which IRDAI publishes every year.

The order that works

  • First, build the emergency fund.
  • Next, buy term and health insurance.
  • Then clear high-interest debt, such as credit card balances.
  • Only then invest for long-term goals.

Worked example

Say your essential expenses are ₹50,000 a month and your EMIs are ₹15,000. Six months of cover is (₹50,000 + ₹15,000) × 6 = ₹3,90,000. For term insurance, suppose your family would need ₹6 lakh a year for 30 years, rising with 6% inflation while the money earns 7%, and you have ₹30 lakh of loans and ₹20 lakh of goals, with ₹5 lakh of savings. That works out to about ₹2.03 crore, so a ₹2.25 crore cover is a sensible round figure.

Frequently asked questions

Should I invest my emergency fund for higher returns?

Keep it in safe, liquid places such as a sweep-in FD or a liquid fund. Its job is to be there when you need it, not to earn high returns.

Is a term plan with return of premium worth it?

It returns your premiums if you outlive the policy, but costs much more for the same cover. Many people do better with a plain term plan, investing the difference.

Do I need term insurance if I am single?

Only if someone depends on your income, such as your parents, or you have loans others would have to repay. Otherwise, health insurance and an emergency fund come first.

Does my employer’s group cover count?

It helps, but it usually ends when you leave the job and is often small. Treat it as extra, and keep your own term plan.
