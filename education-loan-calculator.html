How is an education loan EMI worked out?

An education loan usually has a moratorium: no EMIs during the course and for a short grace period after it. Interest still runs during that time. If you don’t pay it, it is added to the loan when repayment starts, so your EMI is worked out on a bigger amount. Paying just the interest while you study keeps the loan at the amount you borrowed.

Interest during the moratorium = Loan × rate × months ÷ 12
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1) The calculator charges simple interest during the moratorium, as many banks do; some banks compound it, which makes the loan a little larger. P is the loan when EMIs start, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of EMIs.

Worked example

Say you borrow ₹10,00,000 at 10% for a 4-year course with 6 months’ grace (54 months), and repay over 10 years. If the interest is added to the loan, ₹4,50,000 builds up and your EMI is about ₹19,162. If you pay the interest of ₹8,333 a month while studying, the EMI is about ₹13,215.

Ways to reduce the cost

  • Pay the interest while you study if you or your family can. It stops the loan from growing before repayment starts.
  • Claim the tax deduction. Under the old tax regime, the interest you pay on an education loan for higher studies can be deducted from your income for up to eight years, with no upper limit.
  • Prepay when you can. Most banks allow part-prepayment on education loans; it cuts the interest sharply.

Frequently asked questions

What is a moratorium on an education loan?

It is a period, usually the course length plus 6 to 12 months, when you don’t have to pay EMIs. Interest still builds up during this time unless you pay it.

Is interest charged during the moratorium?

Yes. Most banks charge simple interest during the moratorium and add it to the loan when repayment starts, unless you pay it as you go. Some banks offer a small rate cut if you pay the interest during the course.

Can I get a tax deduction on an education loan?

Yes, under the old tax regime. The interest paid on a loan for higher studies for yourself, your spouse or your children can be deducted for up to eight years from when you start repaying, with no limit on the amount. The new regime does not allow it.

How long can I take to repay an education loan?

Banks typically allow up to 10 to 15 years after the moratorium, depending on the loan amount and the bank. A longer period lowers the EMI but increases the total interest.
