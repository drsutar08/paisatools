What is net worth and why track it?

Net worth is the value of everything you own minus everything you owe. It is the simplest single measure of your financial health. Income shows what comes in each month; net worth shows what you have actually built. Tracking it once or twice a year tells you whether you are moving forward.

Net worth = total assets − total liabilities Use today’s market value for property, funds, shares and gold, and the outstanding amount (not the original loan) for loans. Net worth without your home = net worth − home value + home loan, which shows what you have outside the house you live in.

Worked example

Say you have ₹2 lakh in the bank, ₹5 lakh in deposits, ₹8 lakh in EPF and PPF, ₹6 lakh in mutual funds, ₹2 lakh of gold and a home worth ₹60 lakh, which is ₹83 lakh in all. You owe ₹35 lakh on the home loan and ₹3 lakh on a car loan. Your net worth is ₹83 lakh − ₹38 lakh = ₹45 lakh.

Reading the numbers

  • The trend matters most. A net worth that grows every year means your saving and investing are working.
  • Look beyond your home. You cannot easily spend the house you live in, so check your net worth without it too.
  • Watch the debt share. A falling debt-to-asset percentage means you own more of what you have.
  • Keep some of it easy to reach. Enough cash and deposits to cover emergencies stops you selling long-term investments at a bad time.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good net worth for my age?

There is no single right number, because it depends on your income, family and city. What matters is that it grows steadily each year and that you have enough easy-to-reach money for emergencies.

Should I include my home in net worth?

Yes, at today’s market value, minus the home loan. But also look at your net worth without the home, because you cannot easily spend the house you live in.

Should I count EPF and PPF?

Yes. They are your money, even though you can’t withdraw them freely. They are long-term assets meant for retirement.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

Once or twice a year is enough. Use the same method each time so the numbers are comparable.
