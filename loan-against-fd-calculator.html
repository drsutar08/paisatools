How does a loan against FD work?

The bank holds your FD as security and lends you up to about 90% of it. The FD stays in your name and keeps earning interest, and the loan rate is usually 1% to 2% above the FD rate. If you break the FD instead, the bank cuts a penalty and you stop earning interest on that money.

Loan interest = loan × (FD rate + margin) × months ÷ 12
Cost of breaking = loan × FD rate × months ÷ 12 + loan × penalty × months the FD has run ÷ 12 The first part of the breaking cost is the FD interest you give up while the money is out; the second is the penalty, which banks apply as a lower rate on the time the FD has already run. Loan interest is charged on the amount used, here taken as the full loan for the whole period.

Worked example

Say you have a ₹5,00,000 FD at 7% that has run for 2 years, and you need ₹2,00,000 for 6 months. At 9%, a loan against the FD costs about ₹9,000 in interest. Breaking the FD instead loses ₹7,000 of FD interest over those 6 months, plus a 1% penalty on 2 years of about ₹4,000, so ₹11,000 in all. Here the loan is cheaper by ₹2,000.

Points to note

  • Short needs favour the loan: the loan costs only the margin over the FD rate each month, while breaking costs a penalty on all the time the FD has run. The longer the FD has run, the more breaking costs.
  • Overdraft: many banks give this loan as an overdraft, so you pay interest only on what you use, for the days you use it.
  • Tax: FD interest is taxable, so the interest you give up by breaking is worth less after tax, and breaking can cost a little less than shown. Interest on a loan for personal use is not tax-deductible.
  • Rate on breaking: some banks also pay the lower rate for the shorter period the FD actually ran, which makes breaking cost more than shown.
  • Repay on time: if the loan is not repaid, the bank can close the FD to recover it.

Frequently asked questions

How much loan can I get against my FD?

Most banks lend up to 90% of the deposit amount, and some up to 95%. The FD stays in your name and keeps earning interest while it is pledged.

What is the interest rate on a loan against FD?

Usually 1% to 2% above the FD’s own rate. So an FD earning 7% might back a loan at 8% to 9%.

Is it better to take a loan against FD or break it?

For a short need, the loan is often cheaper, because the FD keeps earning and you pay only the margin over its rate. Breaking costs a penalty on all the time the FD has already run, so it gets dearer the longer the FD has run.

Does a loan against FD affect my credit score?

It is usually reported to credit bureaus like other loans, so repaying on time helps your score and missed payments hurt it.
