Section 80C (Now Section 123): Tax-Saving Options Compared

Section 80C is the most used tax deduction in India. In the old tax regime, it lets you reduce your taxable income by up to ₹1.5 lakh a year through certain investments and payments. From FY 2026-27 the Income-tax Act, 2025 calls it Section 123, but the limit and the options are the same. This guide compares the options so you can pick the ones that fit your goals, not just your tax.

Key takeaways

  • The ₹1.5 lakh limit is shared by all the options together, and it applies only in the old regime.
  • Count what you already pay first: your PF contribution, life insurance premiums, home loan principal and children’s tuition fees often use up much of the limit.
  • For new money, choose by lock-in and risk. ELSS has the shortest lock-in (3 years) and equity returns; PPF is safe and tax-free but locked for 15 years.
  • In the 30% slab, using the full limit saves up to ₹46,800 a year, including the 4% cess.

What counts under 80C

  • Your own Employees’ Provident Fund contribution, and any Voluntary PF
  • PPF, and deposits in a Sukanya Samriddhi account
  • ELSS (tax-saving) mutual funds
  • 5-year tax-saver bank FDs and 5-year post office time deposits
  • NSC and SCSS deposits
  • Life insurance premiums for yourself, your spouse or your children
  • Home loan principal repaid, plus stamp duty and registration in the year you buy
  • Tuition fees for up to two children
  • Your own NPS contribution, within the same limit. NPS also gets an extra ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B), now Section 124.

The main investment options compared

  • ELSS: 3-year lock-in, market-linked returns. Gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year are taxed at 12.5% when you sell. Suits people comfortable with equity.
  • PPF: 15 years, 7.1% now, interest and maturity tax-free. Suits safe long-term savings.
  • Tax-saver FD: 5 years at the bank’s rate, interest taxable. Simple, but the lowest return after tax for higher slabs.
  • NSC: 5 years, 7.7% compounded, interest taxable.
  • Sukanya Samriddhi: for a daughter under 10, 8.2% now, tax-free, long lock-in.
  • NPS: locked until 60, market-linked, with the extra ₹50,000 deduction on top of 80C.

How to plan your 80C

  • Add up what already counts: the PF on your salary slip, insurance premiums, home loan principal and tuition fees.
  • Fill the gap with a goal you already have, rather than a product sold only for tax.
  • Invest through the year, by SIP or monthly deposits, instead of a rush in March.
  • Declare it to your employer with proofs, so less TDS is cut from your salary.

Check which regime suits you first

The new regime, the default since FY 2023-24, has lower rates but allows no 80C deduction. If your total deductions are small, the new regime may leave you with less tax anyway, so compare both with the income tax calculator before you invest just to save tax.

Worked example

Say you are in the 30% slab under the old regime. Your PF contribution is ₹72,000 a year and you pay a ₹20,000 life insurance premium, so ₹92,000 of the limit is already used. Putting another ₹58,000 into PPF or ELSS fills the ₹1.5 lakh. The full ₹1.5 lakh saves ₹46,800 in tax (30% plus 4% cess). In the 20% slab it would save ₹31,200, and in the 5% slab only ₹7,800, which is why the 80C choice matters most for higher earners.

Frequently asked questions

What is the new section number for 80C?

Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from FY 2026-27. The ₹1.5 lakh limit and the eligible investments are the same.

Can I claim 80C in the new tax regime?

No. The new regime does not allow the 80C deduction. Your employer’s NPS contribution (old Section 80CCD(2), now Section 124) is one of the few deductions it does allow.

Which 80C investment has the shortest lock-in?

ELSS mutual funds, at 3 years. Tax-saver FDs and NSC lock your money for 5 years, and PPF for 15.

Is the ₹1.5 lakh limit per person?

Yes, per taxpayer. A couple can each claim up to ₹1.5 lakh, for example for their own PPF accounts or as co-borrowers repaying a home loan.
