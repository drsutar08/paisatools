How does NSC work?

The National Savings Certificate is a five-year savings bond sold at post offices and backed by the government. Interest is added every year and compounds, but you receive it only at maturity, together with your investment. The rate is reviewed every quarter, and the rate on the day you buy stays fixed for the full term.

Maturity value = Amount × (1 + rate)5 Interest compounds once a year. Interest for the first four years is treated as reinvested, so it can be claimed again under Section 80C in the old regime. Fifth-year interest is paid out at maturity.

Worked example

Say you invest ₹1,00,000 in NSC at 7.7% a year. After five years it grows to about ₹1,44,903, which means ₹44,903 of interest. The first four years’ interest, ₹34,544, counts as reinvested and can be claimed under Section 80C in the old regime.

Key rules

  • How much: at least ₹1,000, in multiples of ₹100, with no upper limit.
  • Term: five years. You cannot normally cash it early; exceptions include the death of the holder or a court order.
  • Tax: the investment qualifies for deduction under Section 80C in the old tax regime, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit. The interest is taxable each year, but interest for years one to four can also be claimed under 80C because it is treated as reinvested.
  • Other uses: an NSC can be pledged as security for a loan, and it can be transferred in certain cases.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current NSC interest rate?

7.7% a year for October to December 2026, compounded yearly and paid at maturity after five years. The government reviews the rate every quarter, but the rate on the day you buy stays fixed for your certificate.

Is NSC interest tax-free?

No. The investment qualifies for deduction under Section 80C in the old regime, up to the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit. Interest is taxable, but interest for the first four years is treated as reinvested, so it can also be claimed under 80C. Fifth-year interest is taxable with no deduction.

Can I cash an NSC before five years?

Generally no. Early encashment is allowed only in limited cases, such as the death of the holder or an order from a court.

Is there a maximum NSC investment?

No. The minimum is ₹1,000, in multiples of ₹100, and there is no upper limit. The Section 80C deduction, however, is capped at ₹1.5 lakh a year across all eligible investments.
