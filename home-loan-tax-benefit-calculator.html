What tax benefits does a home loan give?

For a home you live in yourself, the old tax regime gives two deductions every year. The interest you pay can be deducted up to ₹2 lakh a year. The principal you repay counts towards the ₹1.5 lakh limit that you share with other tax-saving investments. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 these are now Sections 22 and 123; many people still know them as Sections 24(b) and 80C.

Tax saved = (interest deduction + principal deduction) × your slab rate × 1.04 Interest deduction = interest paid in the year, up to ₹2,00,000. Principal deduction = principal repaid, up to whatever is left of the ₹1,50,000 limit after your other investments. The 1.04 adds 4% cess. Interest and principal for each year come from the standard EMI schedule.

Worked example

Take a ₹40 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years. In the first year you pay about ₹3,36,946 in interest and repay ₹79,609 of principal. Only ₹2,00,000 of that interest can be deducted, and if you already invest ₹50,000 elsewhere, ₹79,609 of the principal fits in the ₹1.5 lakh limit. In the 30% slab that saves about ₹87,238 in tax under the old regime.

Points to note

  • New regime: there is no deduction for a self-occupied home, so the tax saved is nil. Compare your total tax under both regimes before you choose.
  • Joint loans: each co-owner who also repays the loan can claim deductions within their own limits.
  • Under construction: interest paid before you get possession can be claimed in five equal parts from the year construction is completed, within the same ₹2 lakh limit.
  • Rented-out homes follow different rules, so this calculator covers only a home you live in.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax can I save on a home loan?

Under the old regime, up to ₹2 lakh of interest a year plus principal within the ₹1.5 lakh investment limit. In the 30% slab, that can save up to about ₹1.09 lakh a year including cess.

Can I claim home loan benefits in the new tax regime?

Not for a home you live in. The new regime does not allow the interest or principal deduction for a self-occupied home, though it has lower tax rates.

Can both husband and wife claim the home loan deduction?

Yes, if both are co-owners and both repay the loan. Each can claim interest up to ₹2 lakh and principal within their own ₹1.5 lakh limit.

Do stamp duty and registration count?

Yes, in the year you pay them, within the same ₹1.5 lakh limit as principal repaid and other investments, under the old regime.
