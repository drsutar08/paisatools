How does the comparison work?

Buying and renting both cost money, so the fair question is where you end up. When you buy, you pay the down payment and buying costs, then the EMI and upkeep, and you own a home that may rise in value. When you rent, you keep that upfront cash invested and pay rent instead. Each month, whoever spends less invests the difference. The calculator adds it all up for the years you choose.

Wealth if you buy = home value − loan left + money invested
Wealth if you rent = upfront cash + monthly savings, invested Interest and returns are worked out monthly. Rent rises once a year. Maintenance and property tax are a share of the home’s value. Tax benefits on the home loan and tax on investment returns are not included.

Worked example

Take a ₹80 lakh home with 20% down, 7% buying costs and a 20-year loan at 8.5%, against renting a similar home for ₹25,000 a month that rises 5% a year. If the home grows 5% a year and savings earn 10%, after 15 years buying leaves you with about ₹1,39,24,307 and renting with about ₹2,08,70,525.

What tips the balance

  • Rent compared with price. In many Indian cities a year’s rent is only about 2% to 4% of the home’s price. Low rent makes renting and investing look better.
  • Home value growth. The higher the growth you assume, the better buying looks. Try a few rates; property prices do not rise every year.
  • Investment discipline. Renting only wins if you actually invest the difference every month.
  • How long you stay. Buying costs are high, so buying usually needs many years to pay off.
  • Things money can’t measure. Security, freedom to renovate and not having to move matter too, and the calculator can’t value them.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to rent or buy a house in India?

It depends on prices, rent, interest rates and how long you will stay. Where rent is low compared with the price, renting and investing the difference often builds more wealth over 10 to 15 years. Buying can win when you stay longer and home values grow well.

What return should I use for invested savings?

Use a realistic figure for where you would invest. A mix of equity and debt funds may earn more than a deposit over the long term, but returns are not guaranteed.

Are home loan tax benefits included?

No. Under the old tax regime you may claim deductions on home loan interest and principal, which would improve the buying side. The new regime does not allow them for a self-occupied home.

How much do buying costs add?

Stamp duty and registration are usually around 5% to 8% of the property value, depending on the state, plus any brokerage and legal fees. These costs are not recovered when you sell.
