How the tax is worked out

How is tax on shares and equity funds worked out?

When you sell listed shares or units of an equity mutual fund for more than you paid, the profit is a capital gain. How it is taxed depends on how long you held the investment. These rates apply for FY 2026-27 under the Income-tax Act, 2025; Budget 2026 kept them unchanged.

Short-term (held 12 months or less): tax = gain × 20%
Long-term (held more than 12 months): tax = (gain − unused ₹1.25 lakh exemption) × 12.5% 4% health and education cess is added to the tax. The ₹1.25 lakh exemption is shared by all your long-term equity gains in the financial year. The calculator does not add surcharge, which applies only at high incomes.

Worked example

Say you bought equity fund units for ₹5,00,000 in April 2023 and sold them for ₹8,00,000 in November 2026. You held them for more than 12 months, so the ₹3,00,000 gain is long-term. The first ₹1,25,000 is exempt, and 12.5% tax plus 4% cess on the remaining ₹1,75,000 comes to ₹22,750. You keep ₹7,77,250.

Points to note

  • Old investments: for shares or equity funds bought before 1 February 2018, the cost can be taken as the higher of what you paid and the price on 31 January 2018 (but not more than the sale price).
  • Losses: a capital loss is not taxed. It can be set off against other capital gains under the rules, and unused losses can be carried forward if you file your return on time.
  • Low total income: if your other income is below the basic exemption limit, residents can use the unused limit to reduce taxable capital gains.
  • Other assets: property, gold, debt funds and unlisted shares follow different rules, so this calculator does not cover them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the tax on long-term capital gains from shares?

For listed shares and equity mutual funds held for more than 12 months, gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 12.5%, plus 4% cess. Gains up to ₹1.25 lakh a year are tax-free.

What is the tax on short-term capital gains from shares?

If you sell within 12 months of buying, the whole gain is taxed at 20%, plus 4% cess. There is no exemption for short-term gains.

Is the ₹1.25 lakh exemption per sale?

No. It is a yearly limit for all your long-term gains on listed shares and equity funds together. Enter gains you have already made this year so the calculator uses only what is left.

Does SIP have a different holding period?

Yes, in effect. Each SIP instalment is treated as a separate purchase, so each one becomes long-term 12 months after its own date. When you sell, the oldest units are treated as sold first.
